# Building Into Nothing

*This essay reads the dissolution mechanism identified in the framing essay "What the Engine Is For." It is readable as a standalone piece following the framework document and, ideally, the transparency threshold essay.*

## Character Construction and the Object Thin Procedure Dissolves

The fiction engine is not indifferent to character. The opposite is true: the paradigm is optimized for character in a specific and precise sense. It produces a character who is present inside the fiction — who has a history, a reputation, a set of capabilities and limits that shape what the fiction will accept from them — and who changes through consequence rather than through deliberate construction. The character accumulates. They do not build.

That distinction is the source of the mismatch this essay examines. Character construction as a primary engagement requires something the fiction engine cannot provide, not because the paradigm refuses to engage with character but because it dissolves the structural object that construction requires. The experience is not degraded. The object is absent.

## Two Kinds of Character

The argument depends on a distinction the framing essay introduces as a mechanism of mismatch: dissolution. To see what gets dissolved and why, it is necessary first to name the two objects.

*Character-as-fiction* is a set of fictional facts that establish who this person is inside the shared situation. What they are capable of and what they cannot do. What relationships they hold and what standing they have. What their history has made of them and what the fiction's other inhabitants expect from them. These facts shape what the character can plausibly attempt and what outcomes will be accepted as consistent. They accumulate over play through consequence: a reputation established by action, a relationship formed under specific conditions, a physical or psychological state that developed from prior events. The character becomes a richer fictional object as play continues, but no one designed it that way. The fiction produced it.

*Character-as-system* is a set of mechanical properties that interact with each other and with the resolution procedure in ways that reward understanding, planning, and optimization. The properties have numerical or categorical values. Their interactions produce effects the player can discover through play and exploit through deliberate choice. Building the character means making decisions about these properties that produce a mechanically distinct profile: not just a differently described character but a genuinely different mechanical object, one that plays differently because of how its properties interact. The player is not finding out who their character becomes. They are designing what their character will be.

These are different objects. A fiction engine produces the first. Character construction as a primary engagement requires the second. The distinction is not between players who prefer narration and players who prefer mechanics, or between roleplaying and system mastery as orientations to the game. It is between two structural objects, one of which a fiction engine cannot supply.

## Why Opacity Is Load-Bearing

A system worth building into must have depth that is not immediately visible from inspection. Properties whose interactions are not obvious on first reading. An optimization space that opens as the player engages more closely with the design. Effects that reward sustained attention rather than full legibility from the outside.

Opacity is load-bearing because without it there is nothing to discover. A character system whose properties and their interactions are immediately apparent from reading the sheet offers the player no direction to build toward. The exploration that construction-as-engagement requires cannot occur if the system is already fully legible. Construction engagement requires something to be worked out, discovered through use, assembled from choices whose combined effects only become clear once made.

The fiction engine's transparency commitment produces exactly this legibility, and produces it deliberately. A fiction engine character — fictional descriptors, minimal tracking, conditions expressed as diegetic states rather than numerical flags — is designed to be clear on inspection. "She is a skilled fencer with a reputation for reckless courage and a crippling debt to the wrong people" is a complete fictional picture. A reader encounters it and knows who this person is. There is nothing hidden in it. No mechanical interactions the player can optimize toward. No build direction to pursue over the next six sessions.

This is the design working correctly. The fiction engine solves opacity as a problem: it wants the character to be immediately legible so the player can attend to the fiction rather than to the character system. Construction engagement requires the problem to remain unsolved. The dissolution mechanism runs in both directions — the fiction engine cannot provide the opaque system, and a design that provides the opaque system has given up the transparency commitment that allows the fiction engine's resolution procedure to sit below the transparency threshold.

## The Advancement Problem

Fiction engine advancement — where it exists — tends to be minimal and fictional: new capabilities described in fictional terms, new relationships established through play, new reputation accrued through the consequences of past decisions. A character who began as a cautious negotiator and survived three violent confrontations has changed, and the change can be registered as a new fictional fact. But it is not a mechanically different profile. The player did not design toward it. It is what the fiction produced.

Construction engagement requires advancement that changes the mechanical profile in ways the player can plan before they happen. Session 20 should offer the player a mechanical identity that was unavailable at session 1, traceable to specific build decisions made over the intervening sessions. The player is not discovering what their character became; they are building what their character will be. The intention precedes the outcome. That precession is what makes it construction rather than accumulation.

The fiction engine produces accumulation without precession. A character who survives twenty sessions inside a fiction engine has a history whose weight is genuinely irreplaceable. Relationships tested by specific events. Decisions whose consequences are woven into the causal tissue of the fiction. A reputation that other characters in the fiction respond to because of specific things that actually happened. None of this was designed; all of it was produced by play. That production is not a failure to provide the built mechanical profile. It is a different achievement, valuable on its own terms. But it is not what the player who wants to engage in character construction is seeking, and knowing this is not a preference question.

## Why This Is Structural Rather Than Preferential

The common framing treats this as a compatibility issue: some players prefer deep character systems, and the fiction engine is not designed for those players. The framing is not wrong, but it misses the sharper point.

The fiction engine does not discourage character construction or design against it as a preference. It dissolves the precondition for it. A player who wants to engage in character construction inside a fiction engine cannot do so not because the paradigm is hostile to that engagement but because the object the engagement requires — a mechanical system rich enough to build into, opaque enough to reward sustained exploration, deep enough that session 20 reveals properties session 1 did not — does not exist inside the paradigm. You cannot build into a system that has nothing to build into.

The mechanism is dissolution rather than prohibition. A design that prohibits character construction leaves the player frustrated because what they want is present but inaccessible. A design that dissolves the structural precondition leaves the player without a target: there is no opaque system to engage with, no optimization space to explore, no mechanical depth to discover. The absence is not a locked door. It is an empty room.

This means the problem cannot be solved by encouraging players to adopt a different orientation. A fiction engine player who is told to "appreciate the accumulated fictional weight rather than seeking mechanical depth" is being asked to want a different thing. That is not a design solution. It is an admission that the design does not provide what the player is looking for, stated as advice.

## The Hybrid Position

Some designs have tried to combine a fiction engine resolution procedure with a character system opaque enough to support construction engagement. The architecture of these designs is instructive.

The resolution procedure sits below the transparency threshold: thin, fast to internalize, fictional in its outputs. The character system sits above it: persistent between sessions, legible as a mechanical object rather than a purely fictional one, the source of ongoing attentional demand during session preparation and during play whenever mechanical build decisions become relevant. The character sheet is a persistent object of engagement even when the resolution dice are not in play.

This produces a design occupying two threshold positions at once: below the threshold during resolution, above it during character engagement. The seam between them — the moment when the player's attention must shift from the fiction to the character's mechanical properties — is the same architectural feature identified in the Hints and Hijinx deduction phase and in OSG's strategic planning layer. Each of these is a deliberate threshold crossing, contained to a specific moment in the design rather than distributed across play.

The cost is specific. A player attending to their mechanical build is not attending to the current fictional situation. These orientations are not simultaneously available: the attention that reads the fiction as a set of possible character actions and the attention that reads the character sheet as a set of mechanical interactions to optimize are oriented toward different objects. A design that requires both is asking the player to hold two positions at once, and the orientational divergence this produces is precisely what the fiction engine paradigm is built to prevent. The hybrid design puts the failure mode back in, as a deliberate trade for the construction engagement it wants to provide.

This is not an argument against hybrid designs. It is an argument for knowing what the trade is. A design with a fiction engine resolution procedure and a rich character construction system is not a fiction engine with bonus features. It has a seam — between its two positional demands — that requires explicit architectural management. The seam does not disappear through calibration or good facilitation. It remains a feature the design has to own.

## What the Fiction Engine Does Produce Over Time

The comparison should be fair.

A character who has played for twenty sessions inside a fiction engine carries something that a mechanically built character typically does not. The causal tissue connecting prior decisions to current fictional facts. Relationships that formed under specific conditions, tested in specific ways, carrying the weight of what they have been through. A reputation that precedes the character into situations, built from actions whose specific content the other characters in the fiction know and respond to. None of this can be read from a sheet at session 20. It is distributed across the fiction itself, held in common by the participants who were there.

This is not a lesser version of the built mechanical profile. It is a different object. A built mechanical profile is legible to anyone who can read the design's notation — a new player reading the character sheet at session 20 can understand who this character is mechanically. The twenty-session fiction engine character cannot be introduced in a character sheet at all. The character is, in large part, the history the participants share, and someone who was not present for it does not have access to it.

The choice between these objects is a choice about what kind of thing the character is. A designed mechanical object that develops through deliberate construction, whose session 20 state is the result of choices the player made toward it. Or an accumulated fictional entity whose session 20 state is the result of consequences the fiction produced from it. Both are achievable design goals. Neither produces the other as a byproduct. Knowing which one a design is building toward, and which one its players are expecting, is the beginning of designing toward it deliberately — and of being honest about what the other costs to produce.
